# Plan: tightening phylloptim after 0.27.0

Written for: agents picking up work on this package. Each item names what to change, where, what evidence says it is worth doing, and what "done" is. The claims were verified against commit e898a0f (branch `collar-dry-pin-continuity`, 0.27.0) on 2026-11-02; the line counts come from [#206](https://github.com/traitecoevo/phylloptim/issues/206), measured on master at 0.26.2. Where an item says "measure first", the number quoted came from an audit agent's prototype and has to be reproduced before it is acted on.

## Rules that apply to every item

- **A change to any model goes through `inst/model-registry.yml`**, and `tests/testthat/test-registry.R` has to agree with the code afterwards. The registry is a record, not a check: nothing fails when the two drift, so the agent is the check.
- **No slowdown unless it is essential, and every claim of cost is measured interleaved.** Run `tests/cpp/bench_solve` on master and on the branch alternately, three times each, on an idle machine, and quote both; [`.claude/measurements.md`](.claude/measurements.md#benchmark-method) has the method. For plant-facing changes also time a plant stand (the seasonal stand in plant's `test-scm.R` is the near-end-heavy one).
- **Every trait has gradient infrastructure in R.** A new differentiable parameter needs its `par_*` slot before `par_PPFD`, a row in `.solve_price_fields` and `.gradient_owned_pars` if it is model-owned, a write in `.gradient_setter`, and a row in `.level_defaults` if it has a default. The `fitting` vignette's "Fitting a different collection of parameters" says why.
- **Bit-identical first.** A refactor that claims to change no number is asserted on all three goldens (`make -C tests/cpp build && ./test_golden && ./test_primitives`) before anything else is believed; a comment-only change is additionally shown bit-identical on the compiled objects. Reductions (`TF24_floor` at `lambda_o = 0` is `TF24`) are asserted bit for bit, not to a tolerance.
- **Comments carry contracts and hazards, not history.** State the reason, never the issue number. The nineteen hazards in `.claude/hazards.md` are the list; a new one is appended as 20.
- Open an issue when you pick an item up, and close it from the PR. Measurements, alternatives rejected and suite counts go in the PR's first comment, not its body ([plant-meta's commit-message rule](https://github.com/traitecoevo/plant-meta/blob/main/governance/commit-messages.md)).

## Order, and why

1. Two latent bugs (section A). Cheap, and each is a wrong number waiting for a caller.
2. The contracts CI does not hold (section B), including the length checks #206 asks for. Cheap, and they stop sections C and D from regrowing.
3. Shorter comments and NEWS (section C, #206 options 1 and 2). The largest reduction, no behaviour change, and it makes every later item's diff readable. Do it before the speed and design work, not after, so those diffs are not buried in comment churn.
4. The measured speed win (section D).
5. Split the header (section E, #206 option 3). No lines saved, but the design pass in F reads one subsystem at a time.
6. One roof for models and gradients (section F), which holds #206 options 4 and 5 and the audit's infrastructure findings. Its design is written below and needs the owner's approval, including the decisions at its end, before any code.

**One PR per group, not per item** (decided 2026-11-02): A; B1–B4, B6 and C4; C1–C3, C5 and B5 (one commit per file for C1, still no code change, shown bit-identical as a whole); D; E; F's design; then one per step of F's order. Each group opens one issue naming its items, and a group whose predecessor has not merged is stacked on it.

## A. Latent bugs — done in 0.27.1 (#207)

**A1. `evaluate_psi_stem` leaves `lambda_emergent_` stale.** `inst/include/phylloptim/leaf_model.hpp`, `Leaf::evaluate_psi_stem` (the stem-route evaluation at a prescribed stem potential, ~line 6358) writes every output of the point except `lambda_emergent_`, so a caller reading the shadow price after it gets the previous solve's. Every other exit of a solve writes it (`lambda_collar_for<K>()` or `lambda_for<K>()`); hazard 8 says why every path must. Done when the function writes it, `test_leaf.cpp`'s stale-state sweep covers the stem route's prescribed evaluation, and the goldens are bit-identical.

**A2. `leaf_batch()` never checks the prices.** `R/gradient-batch.R`, `leaf_batch()` (line 85) takes the model-owned prices as arguments and seats them without `.check_prices()`, which `leaf_solve()` (`R/leaf-model.R`, line 1083) calls on the same arguments. A batch built with a price its model does not own, or a missing one, fails later or not at all. Done when the batch calls the same check and `test-gradient-batch.R` asserts the same refusal `leaf_solve()` gives.

**A3. The C++ fixtures' `stem_P50`.** `tests/cpp/test_transpose.cpp:398`, `test_leaf.cpp:1727` and `:4863` hard-code `stem_P50 = 3.4287` where `leaf_traits()` defaults to 3.4 and plant's trait-built default is 2.889. Done when each fixture either reads the default or says in one line why it does not.

## F. One roof for models and gradients — design proposed, awaiting approval

This was #206's options 4 and 5, its further candidates, and the audit's findings on where a model or a gradient is bespoke rather than generic. The design below replaces the item list that stood here; the labels F1 to F11 are kept so references to them still resolve. No code is written for F until the owner approves this section, including the decisions at its end.

**Decided before the design pass** (2026-11-02, by the package owner). These are requirements, not options for the design to weigh:

- **Every curve runs on both routes**, collar and stem. Neither route is retired, and no curve is restricted to one.
- **Every curve has gradients**: through the R and C++ trait gradient, and through the derivative surface plant differentiates. The surface's restriction to `TF24` and `TF24_floor` (F5) is a gap to close, not a restriction to document.
- **The R bindings stay.** F2 is not a cut of the bound surface; where bindings are per curve or per route, they derive from the same definition as the curve or route.
- **The way there is common infrastructure, not more code per curve**: one definition of what a curve is, from which both routes, the gradients, the bindings and the registry entry follow. A design that meets the first three by adding per-curve code has missed the point.

### What is true now

Measured on 2026-11-02 at the head of #217 and plant `origin/develop` cc2ef400 (which pins phylloptim 0.26.2). Several counts in the item list this replaces were stale; these are the current ones.

- **A tenth curve would touch, today:** the enum and `n_cost_curves`; `with_curve()` and `curve_name()`; 11 tables ending in `static_assert(..., "unhandled CostCurve")` (ten in `leaf_cost_curves.hpp`, one in `closed_form.hpp`); 11 `K == CostCurve::X` tests outside those tables; 6 sites where a curve is hard-wired (`shadow_cost`, ProfitMax's `|A|max` reading TF24, `find_root_collar_psi()`, the batch's `route_seat`, the surface billing `par_TF24_floor_lambda_o`); about 11 R sites keyed by curve or price (`.solve_price_fields`, `.gradient_owned_pars`, `.gradient_setter`, `.level_defaults`, `leaf_batch()`'s arguments, `align-curves.R`); the `par_*` slots and their lists in `gradient.hpp` and `cpp-tests.yml`; four or more registry entries; and seven hand-written per-curve lists in `test_leaf.cpp` plus `test_golden.cpp`'s own `Solver` enum. `TF24_floor`'s own PR touched 32 files.
- **Solves:** every curve already runs on both routes. `optimise()` dispatches the seated (curve, route) through `with_curve()`; the stem route is one template, `optimise_psi_stem_single<K>`, for all nine.
- **Trait gradient:** `leaf_gradient()` and the batch cover every curve on the stem route and only TF24 on the collar route (`model = "collar"` means TF24). R reaches any other curve's collar route only through `$set_model()`. The two disagree in two places (#219): `leaf_gradient(model = "collar")` on a leaf seated on another curve solves TF24 and differentiates the seated curve, and the batch's finite-difference arm ignores a caller's `step`. `gradient_golden.tsv` holds 20 rows, all TF24 on the collar route.
- **Derivative surface:** TF24 and TF24_floor only, by four `static_assert`s. It bills TF24's scalar cost kernel and writes TF24_floor's extra term inline. `cost_deriv`, `cost_deriv_upstream`, `profit_psi_stem_for`, `lambda_for`, `lambda_collar_for`, `benefit_link_deriv` and `dprofit_energy_balance_term` are `double`-only. plant differentiates TF24_floor alone, on the collar route (TF24 is TF24_floor at a zero price).
- **Costs and their slopes are written by hand per curve.** Only TF24's slope is a tangent of its cost kernel; CF77's, JS22's, CMax's, SOX's, JW26's, LeastCost's and ProfitMax's are closed forms, and some read the conductivity where a tangent of the tabulated integral would read the table's slope. So a slope derived from a generic cost by a tangent is not the same number, and the design keeps each curve's expressions.
- **Profit:** nine `profit_psi_stem_*` bodies in two shapes, a difference `A - C` (TF24, CF77, JS22, CMax, TF24_floor, identical but for the cost call) and a gain (`A·g` for SOX and JW26, `A/W` for LeastCost), plus ProfitMax's normalised difference.
- **ProfitMax (F4):** 14 name-branches, ten of them table arms. The other four are properties: it seeds its normalisers before a search (collar and stem, two sites), it has its own degenerate exit, and its cost reads leaf temperature (as LeastCost's does).
- **Bindings (F2):** 155 active fields and 77 methods on `Leaf`. 43 belong to one curve and mostly follow one pattern: a parameter field, `hydraulic_cost_<C>` (or `<c>_reduction`), `profit_psi_stem_<C>` and `lambda_<C>`. TF24's cost and profit are suffixed `_TF`, CF77 has no λ method, LeastCost binds only its parameter. `$lambda`, `$lambda_molar` and `$lambda_multilayer` return TF24's λ whatever curve is seated; `$lambda_emergent_` is the seated curve's. plant binds the C++ class in its own YAML, so its contract is the C++ member names (hazard 7), not this package's YAML.
- **Closed form (F6):** no consumer outside this repository. plant does not call it, `leaf_solve()` cannot reach it, and R reaches it only through `$set_model(curve, "stem", "closed")`. It covers TF24, CF77 and TF24_floor, refuses the collar route, layered supply, the energy balance, thermal damage and `g_min != 0`, and is never under a gradient.
- **Medlyn (F8):** not a `CostCurve`, and nothing in the R surface is built on it. `g1_eff` reads no Medlyn member (it is `ci`, `ca` and the deficit); `ci_abs_tol` reaches only the Medlyn solve. plant binds six Medlyn fields and three methods, uses them only in its tests, and passes `ci_abs_tol` to the constructor.
- **F9:** `damage::Dual` is used once in production (`leaf_damage.hpp`, a forward pass per input). Its operations are ones odelia's `tangent_scalar` already provides, with `to_passive` for `val()` (inferred, not yet compiled). There is no damage-kind enumeration; the overlap in meaning is between `gradient::Status` (Interior, Pinned, NoGradient, Error, Prescribed, Refused) and `OperatingPointKind`, which classify different things: the derivative and the point.
- **F10:** the enum has nine curves and `cost_curve_names()` is derived from it. "Eight" stands in `the-models.Rmd`, `phylloptim.Rmd`, `derivative-surface.Rmd` and `inst/RcppR6_classes.yml`'s roxygen, and "seven" in `R/gradient.R`. The guide, the README and `COMPARISON.md` already say nine.
- **F11:** 18 writes of `operating_point_kind_`, nine of them classifying exits of the collar solve; the temperature finite difference written twice (`temp_deriv` and inline in `dprofit_energy_balance_term`, kept apart because sharing once moved the gate-off golden); the `A'` tangent block written twice (collar and stem); the closed form's Newton loop written twice.

### The design: a curve is one definition

**A curve is a specialisation `Curve<CostCurve::K>`**, all nine in `leaf_cost_curves.hpp`, holding everything about it in one place:

1. **Identity:** its name. `curve_name()` and `set_model()`'s lookup read it.
2. **Parameters:** the `par_*` slots it owns, each with its bound field name and its default. The aligned defaults (#156) live here and nowhere else in C++.
3. **Properties:** the rows of today's tables and ProfitMax's four branches. That is its benefit link (`Identity`, `Log`, `Scaled`), its objective's shape (difference, gain, normalised), whether its cost reads the upstream potential, whether its domain ends at `psi_crit`, whether its cost reads leaf temperature, whether it prepares normalisers before a search and exits on a degenerate one, and whether it has a closed form.
4. **Its cost, the cost's slope in `psi_stem` and, where it reads one, in the upstream potential, all on any scalar.** Each is the curve's existing expression, moved and templated on the scalar and on a context that reads the seated members at `double` and the `leaf_pars` pack at a tangent or adjoint. At `double` it is the same arithmetic in the same order, so the goldens do not move.
5. **λ**, from the slope and the link where the formula is the generic one, and an override where a curve needs its own (ProfitMax's normalised λ).

**A concept checks each specialisation is complete**, so a missing piece is a compile error naming the piece, in place of eleven `static_assert` arms. `with_curve()` stays the one runtime switch, and the routes, the gradients and the bindings read `Curve<K>` instead of the tables.

What derives from the definition:

- **Both routes** read `Curve<K>` for cost, slope, λ and properties; neither holds per-curve code. `find_root_collar_psi()` solves the seated curve (F3), as `optimise()` already does.
- **Profit:** one evaluator per shape replaces the nine `profit_psi_stem_*` bodies (F11). The bound per-curve methods stay as one-line calls into it.
- **Trait gradient:** a model is a (curve, route) pair, for every curve on both routes, in `leaf_gradient()` and the batch. The batch's `route_seat` and R's `.gradient_route` seat the pair, which closes #219's first item. ProfitMax's re-seat between perturbations follows from its "prepares normalisers" property, not its name.
- **One gradient (F1):** `leaf_gradient()` becomes the batch with one observation on the caller's leaf, so no `Leaf` is built when `x` is given (`test-cost.R`'s count holds). `R/gradient.R` keeps argument handling and the output's shape. The bit-for-bit invariant, `rounded()` and "the reference implementation" retire from the guide. Before it, `gradient_golden.tsv` gains rows for every curve on both routes, the damage layer and a prescribed point: it becomes the guard that the R replica's equality test was.
- **Derivative surface (F5):** `operating_collar<K, S>` and `billed_outputs_at<K, S>` bill `Curve<K>`'s cost and link at the consumer's scalar; the `static_assert`s and the inline TF24_floor arm go. A gain objective differentiates through its link like any other. ProfitMax's normalisers need rows of their own: `k_span` is closed form in the traits, and `|A|max` is a maximum over `psi`, whose derivative is `dA/dtheta` at its own argmax (plus the argmax's motion where that argmax is an end that moves with the traits).
- **R and the bindings (F2, F10):** one bound C++ function lists every curve's parameters (curve, slot, field, default), as `cost_curve_names()` already lists the curves. `.solve_price_fields`, `.gradient_owned_pars`, `.level_defaults` and `.gradient_setter`'s write read it. Generic bound methods taking the curve's name (its cost, profit and λ at a stem potential) cover every curve, LeastCost included. The per-curve methods stay bound but are frozen: no new curve adds one. A test asserts that every parameter in the definition is bound, registered and in the R list, as `test-registry.R` asserts every bound field is registered. The documents' curve counts are asserted against `length(cost_curve_names())`.
- **The registry** records each curve's equations against `Curve<K>`'s members, so its `code:` pointers follow the definition.

**After F, a new curve is:** one enumerator (and `n_cost_curves`), one arm in `with_curve()`, one `Curve<K>` specialisation, a `par_*` slot per parameter (appended before `par_PPFD`, hazard 13), its registry entries, and a bound field per parameter. Nothing in a solver, a route, a gradient or the R tables changes, and what is missing fails by name: the concept in C++, the bound-parameter test in R. Its tests are a reduction to a parent where one exists, its rows in `gradient_golden.tsv`, and its arm of the surface referee.

### Order

One PR each, stacked when the predecessor is unmerged. Each keeps all three goldens bit-identical unless it says which number moves and why. Each quotes `bench_solve` interleaved against master, because steps 2 to 4 move code on the hot path (hazard 5), and the rule above allows no slowdown that is not essential.

1. **The two traps (F3, #219).** `find_root_collar_psi()` solves the seated curve; R's collar route seats its curve; the batch's finite-difference arm takes the caller's `step`. Results change only for a script that seats a curve other than TF24 and calls the shorthand, and for a batch called with a non-default `step` and the finite-difference method.
2. **The definition, properties and parameters (F4).** The eleven tables, `curve_name()`, `set_model()`'s lookup and ProfitMax's four branches read `Curve<K>`; the concept replaces the `static_assert` arms. No number moves.
3. **Cost, slope and λ on any scalar.** Each curve's expressions move into `Curve<K>` on the scalar and context; `cost_deriv`, `cost_deriv_upstream`, `lambda_for` and `lambda_collar_for` read them. No number moves.
4. **One profit evaluator per shape (F11's profit bodies).** No number moves.
5. **R from the definition (F2, F10):** the bound parameter list, the four R tables reading it, the generic bound methods, the bound-parameter test, `$lambda`'s meaning (decision D4), and the curve counts fixed and asserted.
6. **The trait gradient on both routes for every curve:** the (curve, route) model in R and the batch, the R `route` argument (decision D3), and `gradient_golden.tsv`'s new rows. Adds capability; moves no existing number.
7. **One gradient (F1).** `leaf_gradient()` is the batch with one observation; `R/gradient.R`'s arithmetic and its invariants retire.
8. **The surface for every curve (F5),** in this order, one PR each or grouped by shape: the difference curves (CF77, JS22, CMax, with TF24 and TF24_floor moving onto the same path), the gain curves (SOX, JW26), LeastCost (which reads leaf temperature), ProfitMax (its normalisers' rows). Each curve gets the surface referee on every operating-point kind (B6's) and `test_transpose` rows, and hazard 17's text follows. plant takes it up in a PR of its own when it moves its pin.
9. **F9:** the `damage::` templates on odelia's `tangent_scalar` and `Dual` deleted, if the damage benches are flat. `gradient::Status` and `OperatingPointKind` stay separate, since they classify different things, and the guide says how they map.
10. **F11's remainder:** the duplicated temperature difference (re-measure the golden move that kept it apart), the duplicated `A'` block, and one exit helper for the classifying writes of `operating_point_kind_`. These come after steps 2 to 4, so collapsing them does not bake in today's dispatch.

F7 needs no step: both routes stay for every curve, as decided.

### Decisions for the owner

- **D1. The closed form (F6).** (a) Keep it, frozen: its three curves carry "has a closed form" in their definitions, no new curve needs one, and it stays outside every gradient. (b) Delete it: no consumer outside this repository uses it, and it is 531 lines plus its tests and bench arms. Recommendation: (a), since keeping it frozen costs one property and deleting it is the harder change to undo; (b) if nothing is meant to use it.
- **D2. Medlyn (F8).** It cannot be a curve as written: the USO form is an empirical conductance, not a cost the solve maximises, and no λ maps onto `g1`.
